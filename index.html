THE “V” IN NINETY SECONDS

The V-model is how systems engineering keeps a program honest. Down the left leg, requirements decompose into design: mission needs become an architecture, the architecture becomes subsystems, subsystems become components. At the bottom of the V sits the foundation — for an additively manufactured airframe, materials data, allowables, and credentialed models, because every margin claimed above is arithmetic on numbers generated there. Up the right leg, the program earns its way back: components verified, then assemblies, then the integrated system on the ground, and finally the system in flight. The dashed horizontal lines are the point of the diagram — each definition level is verified by its mirror level. You don't prove a requirement with a component test, and you shouldn't discover a component problem in flight.

In acquisition language: the left leg is SRR / PDR / CDR territory; the right leg is developmental test & evaluation, with the campaign's gates playing the role of TRRs and certification decisions — each gate says "the evidence below is sufficient to buy the risk above." Where the left leg is rich and its mirror thin, or where execution clusters low on the V while flight approaches, the diagram shows you where risk is being carried. Everything here is live: the same requests, additions, removals, and status updates you make in the TREE view move these numbers.

LAYER buttons above set which counts show (CLEAN-SHEET / EXECUTION / SIDE-BY-SIDE = both). Click any level for the full story and its live test list.
